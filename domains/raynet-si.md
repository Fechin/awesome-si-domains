RayNet.si combines the image of a ray with the connected structure of a net. It could support two distinct software businesses: a contextual customer-relationship workspace, or an IT asset-management service. ## Meaning and naming depth

**Ray** suggests illumination, direction and a focused view. **Net** suggests connections, a network and the relationships between its parts. This is a creative compound reading, with six letters that can carry a technical identity without requiring the customer to learn a long abbreviation.

For a CRM, the network is made of people, organizations, conversations and commitments. For IT asset management, it is made of devices, software, owners and services. These are separate launch propositions. Their shared naming idea is the ability to move from scattered records to an understandable account of what is connected and why that connection matters.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Conversation-to-CRM workspaces

Sales teams could turn authorized meeting notes and messages into proposed contact updates, commitments and next actions. An assistant would link each proposed change to the relevant source and let the account owner approve it. Team subscriptions and integrations could make RayNet useful at the point where a conversation needs to become a reliable shared record.

### 2. Customer relationship graphs

Account teams could connect people, organizations, shared projects and interaction histories in a navigable view. A relationship assistant would explain a connection with evidence from the team's own records, helping a new colleague understand the account. Organization licenses could serve complex sales, partnerships and professional-service teams whose relationships extend beyond a single contact entry.

### 3. Pipeline coordination

Sales managers could connect an opportunity's requirements, stakeholders, decisions and unresolved questions to the next meaningful step. Agents would prepare reviewable work queues and explain why a particular question remains open. Seat-based plans and process integrations could position RayNet around the quality of pipeline coordination rather than the volume of automated outreach.

### 4. Account briefing and meeting preparation

Customer-facing professionals could request a source-linked briefing before a conversation. The workspace would organize recent commitments, service history and important changes while preserving the distinction between recorded facts and suggested discussion points. Professional subscriptions and private deployments could give teams useful preparation without making every person reconstruct the account from scratch.

### 5. Customer onboarding handovers

Delivery teams could receive a structured account of agreed outcomes, practical requirements and information still needed from the customer. An assistant would connect sales commitments to onboarding tasks and highlight unanswered questions for review. Team licenses and implementation services could make RayNet a bridge between winning a customer and beginning the actual work.

### 6. Renewal and relationship reviews

Account owners could connect usage evidence supplied by their systems, service history and recorded commitments into a review plan. The workspace would prepare a discussion agenda and organize follow-up responsibilities. Customer-success subscriptions and integration packages could support a distinct product centered on understanding an ongoing relationship before its next commercial decision.

### 7. Reconciled IT inventories

IT teams could connect device records, software observations and ownership data in an explainable inventory. Reconciliation agents would identify duplicates, conflicting assignments and missing fields for a person to resolve. Asset-volume subscriptions and private deployments could provide a clear first product for organizations whose equipment information is spread across several operational tools.

### 8. Software-license workspaces

Procurement and IT teams could relate contracts, installations, entitlements and renewal dates. The assistant would prepare questions where the records do not support a clear conclusion, retaining source documents for review. Organization plans and integration services could turn RayNet into a workspace for making licensing information understandable across technical and commercial teams.

### 9. SaaS spending reviews

Finance and IT teams could group subscriptions by owner, intended purpose and the organizational use recorded in connected systems. An agent would prepare a budget-review packet and identify decisions that need an accountable owner. Subscription-volume plans and managed analysis services could commercialize the coordination around software spending without promising a particular financial result.

### 10. Endpoint lifecycle coordination

Operations teams could connect purchasing, assignment, maintenance, return and retirement records around each device. The workspace would prepare handovers when an employee joins, changes roles or leaves, with unresolved steps visible to the responsible staff. Per-device plans and workflow integrations could make RayNet useful throughout an asset's working life.

### 11. Managed-service client portals

Service providers could maintain separate client inventories, responsibilities and service histories in one organized interface. An assistant would prepare client-specific briefings and handover packets while keeping each organization's context separate. Provider subscriptions and branded portals could support a service business that needs a consistent view across many independently managed environments.

### 12. Dependency and change-impact views

Infrastructure teams could connect applications, equipment, business services and responsible owners before a proposed change. A reasoning layer would explain which recorded dependencies support its assessment and which relationships remain uncertain. Enterprise licenses and specialist integration services could turn RayNet into a collaborative planning workspace for technical change.

## A Super Intelligence product story

A Super Intelligence CRM under RayNet.si could coordinate relationship memory, requirement reasoning and approved follow-through. Its ITAM counterpart could coordinate inventory reconciliation, dependency analysis and lifecycle planning. Each product would need its own audience, data model and launch workflow, while using the same clear naming metaphor.

[NIST's IT asset-management reference design](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/publication/1800-5/VolB/index.html) explains the value of connecting physical and virtual assets to understand their location, configuration and use. That is a concrete foundation for the second route. RayNet's proposed intelligence layer would help people investigate relationships, review evidence and decide what to do next, with changes routed through the organization's approval process.

## Why the name works

RayNet is compact, readable and naturally separable into two visual ideas. A ray can become a focused line or point of attention, while the net can become a relationship map. **“See what connects. Know what comes next.”** works for either route without forcing customer relationships and infrastructure management into one product. RayNet Accounts and RayNet Assets could identify the chosen specialization clearly.
