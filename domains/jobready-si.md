# JobReady.si — Show what someone can do before the next role begins

![JobReady.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/jobready.si.webp)

JobReady.si describes a useful outcome: being prepared for the work ahead. A practice and evidence platform could connect a role's actual requirements with realistic tasks, explanations and portfolio material. Candidates, training providers and employers would use different parts of that journey. A separate software interpretation could apply the same readiness question to an agent before deployment.

## What the name opens up

JobReady joins a concrete destination, a job, with a state of preparation. A job can mean employment, a particular assignment, or a task handed to software. That breadth creates room for human development and machine readiness under the same identity. Ready emphasizes usable capability: can someone complete the assignment, explain their choices, and respond when the conditions change? [O*NET's occupational profiles](https://www.onetonline.org/) provide a real reference for exploring work activities, knowledge, skills, and job requirements; the product possibilities below are proposed applications.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Career-change practice

A career-transition coach could compare a person's demonstrated experience with a target role, design realistic practice tasks and identify missing evidence. Career advisers would help candidates build a credible transition story rather than merely rename old responsibilities with new job keywords. Paid cohorts or coach subscriptions would turn general ambition into visible weekly progress.

### 2. Work-sample portfolios

A work-sample review tool could examine a candidate's project artifacts, ask how decisions were made and connect the answers with role requirements. Portfolios would become inspectable accounts of capability, making the difference between completed work and unsupported self-description easier to understand. Institutional licenses would support a practical record of capability that remains useful after a course ends.

### 3. New-starter preparation

A first-project preparation guide could connect onboarding materials, access requirements and the new starter's assigned work, diagnosing what they need before the first task. Managers would see readiness gaps early and arrange the right introduction, practice or clarification. Employer subscriptions could connect first-week preparation with a longer role-development pathway.

### 4. Interview rehearsal

An interview practice partner could simulate different interviewers, probe the evidence behind an answer and explain how a response addresses the actual question. Candidates would rehearse thoughtful communication and specific examples instead of memorize a universal script. Commercial options include selling preparation packages or coach subscriptions, with progress demonstrated through actual responses.

### 5. Skilled-trade preparation

A trade-training assistant could assemble practice scenarios from approved procedures, tool knowledge and common worksite questions, adapting to the learner's explanation. Trainers would identify which preparation needs a hands-on demonstration or qualified assessment before real work begins. Training centers could license occupation-specific modules and maintain separate pathways for new entrants and experienced workers changing specialties.

### 6. Freelance launch kits

A freelance launch planner could connect a freelancer's skills, desired customers and service boundaries into sample briefs, delivery plans and client questions. New independents would develop an operationally credible offer rather than start with a polished profile and no fulfillment process. A paid toolkit could serve designers, bookkeepers, assistants, or technical consultants through distinct, concrete starter programs.

### 7. Return-to-work support

A return-to-work guide could map a returning worker's prior experience to changed role expectations, sequence refresher tasks and coordinate support preferences. Coaches would build a practical re-entry plan around demonstrated strengths and current circumstances. Employers, community organizations, or individuals could purchase guided cohorts that respect existing experience while targeting specific gaps.

### 8. Internal mobility

An internal development assistant could compare an employee's project evidence with an internal opportunity, propose stretch assignments and identify a suitable mentor. Managers would see a concrete development path with observable milestones rather than a vague readiness label. The commercial model could combine workforce subscriptions with paid implementation for each job family.

### 9. Simulation marketplaces

A work-simulation service could generate role-specific scenarios, vary conditions and explain how a participant's choices affect the outcome. Training providers would offer repeatable practice experiences whose evaluation criteria remain visible to learners and employers. The brand's readiness promise connects all of them while contributors retain a recognizable area of expertise.

### 10. Agent deployment readiness

An agent release reviewer could test an agent against realistic requests, permission boundaries and recovery scenarios, assembling evidence for a release decision. Operators would understand where the system can work independently and where supervision or additional tooling remains necessary. Scenario suites, tool-permission checks, exception handling, and replayable runs could support developer subscriptions.

## A Super Intelligence product vision

A future JobReady Super Intelligence workspace could begin with a role specification and a learner's actual portfolio. Research, coaching, and simulation agents would propose a practice route, run a realistic client interaction, and review the resulting deliverable against a visible rubric. A mentor could adjust the rubric or reject weak evidence. The distinguishing experience would be a chain from target task to practice to demonstrated performance. A provider could begin with one occupation and expand its library as it acquires specialist teaching expertise.
