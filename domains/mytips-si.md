# MyTips.si — Advice that knows the situation it is meant to help

![MyTips.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/mytips.si.webp)

MyTips.si can mean a personal collection of useful advice, with a separate established gratuity reading of tip. A practical knowledge service could ask about the situation before offering a recommendation, connecting expert material with the user's actual task. Team playbooks, coaching and local guidance use that foundation differently. Gratuity records would be a distinct business, with their own customer and product design.

## What the name opens up

My turns the name toward ownership: an individual's knowledge, chosen recommendations, or earned gratuities. [Tip](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/tip) has several established senses, including a small piece of advice, information, a gratuity, and an extremity. The first two are obvious foundations for a knowledge product; the gratuity sense opens a quite different worker-finance territory. A buyer can decide whether MyTips means advice I receive, expertise I share, or income I record. These are independent business positions, giving the six-letter compound more range than a conventional tips blog.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Expert knowledge library

An expert knowledge assistant could connect a user's specific situation with an expert's approved examples, identify missing context and explain when advice changes. Publishers would turn a collection of tips into a reasoned learning service rather than present every short recommendation as universally applicable. Paid collections, memberships, or expert subscriptions would turn scattered advice into a useful commercial resource.

### 2. Team playbooks

A team playbook guide could retrieve the relevant procedure, compare the employee's situation with its assumptions and ask about exceptions before recommending a step. Organizations would preserve practical know-how while making the context behind an experienced colleague's advice visible. Team subscriptions would preserve experience that otherwise disappears when an individual changes roles.

### 3. Local advice guides

A local advice planner could reconcile a visitor's preferences, available time and practical constraints with locally curated knowledge, producing several feasible plans. Publishers would explain why a suggestion fits the person rather than merely surface the most frequently mentioned venue. Memberships or client packages would support relocation, specialist travel, or community knowledge businesses.

### 4. Gratuity tracking

A gratuity record organizer could connect a worker's recorded gratuities, shift context and stated tracking goals, identify missing entries and explain useful summaries. Hospitality tools would support a clear personal record and preparation for professional advice without inventing a universal rule for every workplace. A consumer subscription or workplace edition would use the established gratuity meaning while remaining distinct from an advice-publishing business.

### 5. Home-maintenance guidance

A household task guide could connect a homeowner's question with the relevant item, service history and approved instructions, proposing an investigation or preparation sequence. Users would receive advice tailored to the actual home and a clear point at which specialist help is appropriate. Family subscriptions or contractor-provided portals could fund a practical personal-maintenance product.

### 6. Coaching follow-through

A coaching follow-through assistant could compare agreed actions, a client's reflections and observed obstacles, asking which assumption no longer fits. Coaches would turn a session's advice into an adaptable practice plan rather than repeatedly issue the same motivational reminder. Practitioner subscriptions could serve study coaches, creative mentors, sports instructors, or productivity advisers.

### 7. Creator recommendation shelves

A creator recommendation guide could connect a creator's explicit selection criteria with audience questions and product context, explaining alternative recommendations. Followers would discover why an item suits a particular use while commercial relationships remain part of the information needed to decide. Memberships, paid collections, or disclosed affiliate recommendations could support a content business that is easier to navigate than a social feed.

### 8. Professional field notes

A field-note research tool could organize practitioners' notes by conditions, failure modes and outcomes, retrieving the example most relevant to a current question. Teams would preserve situational judgment that would be lost if every observation were reduced to a context-free bullet point. Team subscriptions could preserve useful experience that otherwise remains in someone's memory or private messages.

### 9. Travel memory guides

A personal travel guide could connect a traveler's saved places, past experiences and current plans, recommending what deserves revisiting and what no longer fits. A personal guide would learn from the person's actual preferences instead of continually reset to generic destination lists. Independent guides could sell curated collections.

### 10. Skill-specific practice hints

A practice hint tutor could inspect a learner's attempt, identify the earliest consequential misunderstanding and offer a hint that preserves the challenge. Tutors would support progress through reasoning rather than immediately reveal a complete answer that bypasses the learning goal. School licenses or subject subscriptions could support a product focused on practical progress through the difficult step.

## A Super Intelligence product vision

A MyTips SI companion could transform a person's saved notes, selected experts, and stated goals into timely suggestions. It could distinguish a source's advice from the user's own experience, ask whether a recommendation worked, and improve the organization of the personal collection. A professional edition might turn a specialist's methods into a client knowledge library. The intelligence story is relevance and memory: an assistant that knows which practical detail may help now, where it came from, and how the user can evaluate it.
