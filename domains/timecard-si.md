# TimeCard.si — Understand where the work—and the time—went

![TimeCard.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/timecard.si.webp)

A timecard records work, but its value grows when the entry connects to a real assignment and outcome. **TimeCard.si** could serve employers, freelancers, staffing firms, or project teams by making effort understandable: what happened, why it took that long, and what the record means for the next plan.

## What the name opens up

A [timecard](https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=timecard) records working time. The word connects the physical card once associated with a clock to the modern digital record of a person's day. Its two components also invite broader brand interpretations: a card can be a concise unit of information, and time can mean effort, availability, capacity, or a scheduled experience. Those additional readings could support personal planning or agent operations, while the established employment meaning supplies an immediately understandable starting point. The domain is especially clear for buyers who prefer a recognizable category word over an invented software name.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Employee timesheets

A workforce time-recording tool could connect work records, schedules, project context, and agreed policies, identify ambiguous entries, and prepare a clear review queue before approved time flows into payroll processes. Workers could correct their own submissions before an export reaches payroll. Per-worker subscriptions would support a concrete administrative task with clear responsibility at each stage.

### 2. Project costing

A project costing workspace could connect recorded work with project scope, dependencies, and delivery outcomes, explain where effort diverged from expectations, and help managers compare changes before revising a forecast. Managers could compare estimates with approved time and use the lessons in future quotes. Team subscriptions would turn historical hours into useful information about how a business actually delivers work.

### 3. Mobile work records

A mobile work-log assistant could reconcile mobile notes, job assignments, travel context, and completion records, suggest a coherent work log, and send uncertain entries back to the worker for confirmation. Office staff could resolve inconsistencies beside the relevant site context. Per-team subscriptions would suit businesses that need a practical record of work performed away from a conventional desk.

### 4. Freelancer work journals

A freelancer’s work journal could connect client commitments, working notes, revisions, and time records, prepare an understandable account of the engagement, and help the freelancer recognize recurring scope problems. The resulting journal could help prepare invoices and explain progress. A personal subscription could include project budgets, recurring reports, and easy export to existing accounting systems.

### 5. Staffing placement records

A staffing records workspace could reconcile worker assignments, client approvals, shift changes, and contract rules, identify conflicting records, and coordinate resolution before an invoice or payroll handoff is prepared. Exceptions such as changed shifts or missing signoff could have clear owners. Per-agency software subscriptions could serve organizations handling many temporary assignments across several customer sites.

### 6. Capacity planning

A team capacity planner could relate historical effort to current commitments, team capabilities, and uncertain demand, compare capacity scenarios, and explain which dependency most threatens the delivery plan. Staff could edit assumptions about availability. Workspace subscriptions would connect historical effort with future planning without treating every calendar gap as spare capacity.

### 7. Volunteer contribution logs

A volunteer contribution tool could connect volunteer time with activities, community goals, and approved records, help coordinators understand coverage, and prepare meaningful impact narratives without reducing every contribution to a leaderboard. Reports could support planning and recognition. Organization subscriptions or sponsored community editions could serve groups whose valuable work is not captured by a payroll system.

### 8. Study and practice cards

A practice journal could relate time spent to the learner’s actual attempts, feedback, and understanding, recognize unproductive repetition, and recommend a more useful next exercise or study session. Learners could review both the quality of practice and elapsed minutes. Consumer subscriptions or teacher editions would extend the card metaphor into education.

### 9. Equipment rental time

An equipment utilization planner could connect rental intervals with transport, preparation, maintenance, and customer commitments, propose workable booking options, and surface conflicts before equipment is promised for overlapping work. A resource subscription could connect reservations with a clear utilization record. This is a distinct operational business where time belongs to a shared asset.

### 10. Agent execution accounting

An agent execution ledger could trace execution time through tool calls, retries, review steps, and achieved outcomes, compare alternative workflows, and help teams decide which automation is worth continuing. Developers could compare versions and locate expensive waits. Usage pricing or engineering-team licenses would make Timecard a plausible name for understanding digital work.

## A Super Intelligence product vision

A Timecard SI assistant could propose draft entries from a worker's selected calendar events, project activity, and explicit notes. It would show the evidence behind each suggestion and ask the worker to correct the allocation before submission. Managers could inspect unusual entries and compare planned capacity with approved project effort. The useful product is a reviewable record that workers and managers can understand. A team subscription could start with consultants and extend into resource planning, commercial estimation, and the cost of running AI agents alongside human work.
