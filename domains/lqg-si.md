# LQG.si — Connect scientific models with the assumptions behind a decision

![LQG.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/lqg.si.webp)

LQG.si has two distinct scientific readings: linear-quadratic-Gaussian in control engineering and loop quantum gravity in physics. An engineering workspace for estimation, simulation and model comparison is one credible use; a research knowledge platform serves a different audience. The opportunity lies in helping specialists inspect assumptions and follow reasoning, rather than blending these separate subjects into one claimed technical meaning.

## Name roots and associations

LQG has two substantial scientific homes. In control engineering it means linear-quadratic-Gaussian, a framework combining estimation and feedback under a defined model; [MathWorks documentation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/control/ref/ss.lqg.html) provides a practical reference. In physics it can mean loop quantum gravity, the subject of [Carlo Rovelli's research overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9710008). These meanings should remain distinct. Together they make the three-letter domain suitable for an engineering platform, a research community, a specialist publisher, or software exploring the relationship between models, uncertainty, and decisions.

## Ten directions a buyer could build

### 1. Control-system design workspaces

A control-design assistant could connect a control objective, model assumptions, estimator choices and simulation results, proposing comparisons for an engineer to inspect. The workflow would make design trade-offs explicit rather than hide them behind an automatically chosen controller. Team subscriptions could preserve assumptions and experimental context across prototypes, making a design review easier to inspect and a previous result easier to reproduce.

### 2. Robotics estimation experiments

A robotics experiment planner could compare sensor observations, documented noise assumptions and experimental conditions, generating hypotheses for estimation errors. Robotics teams would plan discriminating experiments and maintain a traceable connection between a model adjustment and its observed effect. Laboratory plans could connect proposed controllers with observed behavior while retaining explicit boundaries between simulation performance and evidence from physical equipment.

### 3. Interactive control education

A control-theory learning companion could turn a learner's question about estimation or feedback into interactive examples, then compare their predictions with simulated behavior. Instructors would diagnose conceptual gaps through explanations of changing assumptions rather than a final numerical answer alone. Classroom licenses could include assignments and visual explanations that help students understand the assumptions behind a result rather than simply pressing an optimization button.

### 4. Engineering simulation services

An engineering simulation service could decompose a customer's design question into model variations, parameter sweeps and result checks, organizing the comparisons into an understandable report. Consultancies would spend more attention on interpreting the decision and less on repetitive setup. Consulting fees and software plans could support reviewable reports, parameter studies, and follow-up experiments for the relevant engineering team.

### 5. Autonomous-system evaluation

An autonomy evaluation tool could generate scenarios that challenge an autonomous system's assumptions, inspect behavior traces and identify what caused a failure. Engineering teams would compare recovery strategies and define the evidence needed before expanding the tested operating conditions. Developer plans could retain scenario definitions, configuration versions, and human review, allowing teams to compare specific capabilities without turning a benchmark into an unrestricted autonomy claim.

### 6. Loop quantum gravity publishing

For the loop quantum gravity reading, a literature research assistant could map arguments, mathematical prerequisites and differences between selected papers. Scholars would explore an unfamiliar research thread with citations and open questions, without mistaking a synthesis for a newly established physical result. Memberships and educational collections could support readers ranging from advanced students to researchers following a defined theoretical field.

### 7. Scientific notebook collaboration

A reproducibility reviewer could connect notebook code, parameter choices, data provenance and explanatory notes, identifying where a result cannot yet be reproduced. Research teams would receive a concrete repair plan and an account of which conclusions depend on unresolved steps. Institutional subscriptions could connect a result with its derivation and computational environment, helping collaborators review changes across a long research project.

### 8. Research seminar operations

A seminar knowledge organizer could connect upcoming talks with relevant papers, participant questions and previous discussions, preparing focused reading paths. Research groups would convert a sequence of seminars into a navigable intellectual conversation rather than a calendar archive. University or society plans could preserve the intellectual context of a seminar series rather than leaving useful discussion scattered across individual inboxes.

### 9. A specialist scientific talent network

A scientific collaboration service could compare a research problem's methodological needs with a specialist's published work and stated interests, explaining possible collaborations. Institutions would investigate evidence-backed combinations of expertise instead of rely on a broad subject label. Memberships or recruiting services could match researchers with concrete work while keeping qualifications and project expectations explicit for both sides.

### 10. Intelligent technical literature assistance

A technical-literature assistant could retrieve definitions, compare notation across sources and trace an engineering claim to its assumptions. Practitioners would navigate unfamiliar material with a visible reasoning path and a clear distinction between established methods and speculative extensions. Research subscriptions could help users locate derivations, compare assumptions, and prepare questions for experts without presenting generated explanations as new scientific results.

## A Super Intelligence product story

LQG.si could position Super Intelligence as disciplined reasoning under uncertainty. In engineering, agents prepare models and compare simulations for expert review. In theoretical research, they organize references and derivations with traceable sources. The scientific credibility comes from preserving assumptions and evidence, not from implying the two LQG fields are the same.
