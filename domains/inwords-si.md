# InWords.si — Put a complex idea into words that carry its meaning

![InWords.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/inwords.si.webp)

InWords.si is a natural English phrase about expression through language. A writing workspace could connect evidence, intended audience and the point a person wants to make, helping them produce a clear explanation they can inspect. Speech, interviews, visual descriptions and personal stories offer different inputs; translation and publishing offer different routes to the reader.

## What the name opens up

In words is an ordinary English phrase about expression through language. It can describe a thought, a quantity written out, a personal experience, or an explanation of something previously visual or abstract. That range creates several plausible product families: clear writing, speech transcription, data narratives, accessible explanations, and personal storytelling. Its two short words describe a transformation customers recognize. For text connected to video or audio, [WebVTT](https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/) offers a concrete technical reference for time-aligned text.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Report narratives

A report-writing assistant could connect a report's figures, assumptions and audience questions, drafting an explanation that distinguishes observation from inference. Analysts would inspect which source supports each sentence and revise the interpretation before distributing the narrative. Team subscriptions would support recurring business reporting where clarity and source continuity both matter.

### 2. Learning explanations

An explanation tutor could compare a learner's explanation with the underlying concept, identify the missing connection and offer a more useful analogy. Teachers would move beyond producing simpler text toward discovering exactly what the learner has not yet understood. Publisher licenses or project services would support understandable material that still preserves the important technical distinctions.

### 3. Interview publishing

An interview editor could organize a conversation into themes, preserve distinctive phrasing and suggest follow-up questions where an account remains incomplete. Editors would produce an engaging article whose interpretation stays connected to the speaker's actual words. Professional subscriptions would connect a spoken interview with a carefully prepared written account for its intended audience.

### 4. Speech-to-text notebooks

A speech-to-notes assistant could distinguish an idea, a commitment and a question inside a recording, then propose notes and next steps for approval. Professionals would recover the meaning of a conversation without silently turning every speculative remark into an assigned task. The name clearly describes moving from spoken thought into editable language.

### 5. Personal story services

A personal-story collaborator could connect someone's memories, photographs and interviews, suggest narrative structures and ask about missing context. A writer would help the person express a recognizable voice while preserving conflicting recollections instead of inventing a seamless history. Project fees and print fulfillment would support a business centered on meaningful expression.

### 6. Plain-language business writing

A business writing tool could compare a draft with its audience, intended decision and supporting evidence, identifying jargon and unexplained assumptions. Organizations would receive a clearer document that helps the reader act, rather than a polished rewrite that changes the substance. Agency services or team subscriptions could serve businesses whose information is accurate but unnecessarily difficult to read.

### 7. Translation collaboration

A translation collaborator could compare alternative translations, maintain project terminology and explain where tone or ambiguity changes between languages. Translators would resolve meaningful choices collaboratively instead of treating a fluent machine-generated sentence as an unquestionable final version. Professional subscriptions would position the name around careful expression across languages, with professionals reviewing the result.

### 8. Numbers written out

A quantitative writing assistant could connect a number's units, scale and role in an argument with an accurate verbal explanation. Finance, education and accessibility tools could help readers understand the meaning of a quantity as well as its written-out form. Usage pricing, professional editions, or embedded licensing could turn a literal phrase interpretation into a useful product.

### 9. Letters and occasion writing

A personal-message drafting tool could reason about the relationship, occasion and writer's own memories, proposing several ways to express the intended sentiment. The person would choose wording that sounds like them rather than receive a generic letter with substituted names. Paid collections or assisted-writing sessions could build a consumer business around occasions where people know what they feel but struggle to express it.

### 10. Visual description tools

A visual-description assistant could connect an image or diagram with its surrounding purpose, distinguish essential information from decorative detail and draft a contextual description. Publishers would review language that helps a reader understand the same point the visual is meant to convey. Team subscriptions could serve publishers, museums, and education companies adding useful verbal explanations to visual content.

## A Super Intelligence product vision

An InWords SI system could take a source bundle—an interview, charts, notes, and a specified audience—and prepare several explainable writing routes. A factual summary might stay close to the evidence, while a personal narrative would preserve the speaker's intended voice. Editors could inspect source passages beside proposed sentences and choose what to keep. A strong first business might serve research communicators or specialist publishers. The wider vision is language as a bridge: intelligence that makes difficult material clearer while preserving what the original information actually supports.
