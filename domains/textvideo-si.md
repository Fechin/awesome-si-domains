# TextVideo.si — Carry an idea from the page to the screen

![TextVideo.si brand artwork](../assets/domains/textvideo.si.webp)

**TextVideo.si** states the transformation a customer wants: turn written material into something people can watch and understand. A business could serve product teams, trainers, publishers, and agencies by coordinating the script, scenes, captions, and source facts instead of treating video as a collection of generated clips.

## What the name opens up

Text Video names a direct relationship between two familiar media. It can mean turning written material into video, editing a video through its transcript, or giving video useful text through captions and descriptions. A buyer should choose which relationship the product actually serves; the name is broad enough for all three without confusing them. [WebVTT](https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/) supplies a real standard format for time-aligned text tracks. The larger naming attraction is practical clarity: customers can picture an input, an output, or a bridge between their existing writing and video workflows.

## Business opportunities

### 1. Product video creation

A product-video workspace could connect customer questions, verified product information, and a persuasive narrative, coordinate script and scene specialists, and help reviewers check that the video demonstrates what the product actually does. Clients could inspect the claims and visuals before release. Team subscriptions or production packages would connect an actual product brief with finished promotional material.

### 2. Training video production

A training-video studio could connect procedures, learner misconceptions, and practical examples, plan a visual explanation, and use learner responses to identify which scene or concept should be revised. Subject experts could identify the scene affected by a source change. Institutional licenses or agency services would support material that needs regular, careful maintenance.

### 3. Publisher video editions

A publisher’s video edition could trace an article’s argument and evidence into a visual narrative, distinguish essential claims from optional detail, and preserve source context as the story moves from text to video. Editors could compare the script and proposed scene sequence. Publisher subscriptions or production services would support another format within a coherent editorial operation.

### 4. Transcript-based editing

A transcript-led editing tool could connect transcript meaning with scene continuity, speaker intent, and audience attention, propose structural edits, and explain the narrative consequences before an editor accepts them. Reviewers could leave comments on the exact section. Creator subscriptions or studio seats would support an editing business where language becomes an efficient navigation layer.

### 5. Caption production workspaces

A caption production workspace could reconcile transcript, timing, speaker identity, and intended meaning, identify ambiguous passages, and coordinate a review queue that focuses attention where comprehension is most at risk. Work queues could preserve timing and reviewer decisions. Per-minute processing or team subscriptions would serve a concrete production task distinct from generating complete videos.

### 6. Software demonstration scripts

A software demonstration assistant could connect software tasks, actual interface states, and audience knowledge, generate a coherent walkthrough plan, and detect when a script describes a flow the current product does not support. A change in the interface could be linked to the affected demonstration. Company subscriptions or managed production services could make software explanations easier to maintain.

### 7. Local business video services

A local-business video service could connect service details, customer concerns, available footage, and brand tone, coordinate a useful video story, and adapt it across channels without changing the underlying offer. Clients could choose scenes and review claims before delivery. Project fees or recurring content packages would support an accessible agency business.

### 8. Interactive learning video

An interactive video lesson could relate a viewer’s question and response to the relevant scene, offer an alternate explanation, and choose a meaningful follow-up activity rather than simply replaying the same clip. Instructors could inspect learner responses and revise difficult sections. Education licenses or course-production subscriptions would connect the two media around understanding, not just passive playback.

### 9. Video translation coordination

A video translation workspace could reconcile spoken meaning, subtitles, visual text, cultural context, and timing, coordinate language specialists, and flag adaptations that require a creative decision rather than literal substitution. Terminology decisions could remain attached to the relevant scene. Professional workspaces or project services would address a complex workflow the name can describe naturally.

### 10. Video publishing infrastructure

A video publishing service could connect approved scripts, media assets, version histories, and channel requirements, coordinate delivery tasks, and identify where a source update requires an existing video to change. Documentation and inspectable examples could make integration straightforward. Usage-based pricing would position TextVideo as infrastructure beneath other teams' viewing and production experiences.

## A Super Intelligence product vision

A TextVideo SI production system could connect an approved script to a storyboard, source assets, spoken narration, captions, and a reviewable edit. When the text changes, it could show which scenes and language versions need attention. Producers would retain control over wording, factual claims, footage, and publication. A business could begin with a specific output such as product explainers, then add training or publisher editions. The intelligence vision is coordinated adaptation: maintaining the connection between what a message says and how a viewer experiences it.
